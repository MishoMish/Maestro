Build guide

Маестро

A standalone trainer for musical hearing and memory. It plays a melody by light, sound or both, and you repeat it on 8 piano keys. This guide takes you from a box of parts to a working device, one step at a time.

For the case

You need a 3D printer, about 230 g of black PETG and up to 20 g of white filament for the keys. The case section has the details. The electronics work fine without a case, so you can test everything first.

Handy tools

Small pliers for bending leads and a small screwdriver for the contrast pot and for opening the case's clips. A multimeter helps with finding faults, but you don't strictly need one.

Check your kit first. The "Kit has" counts come from two retailers' listings (Botland, Rapid Online), not from Arduino itself. Kit versions can differ, so count your parts before you buy anything.

How it works

A 16×2 LCD, 8 LEDs, a piezo, a knob and 8 keys would need more pins than an Uno has if every key had its own. The trick is that all 8 keys share one pin.

The resistor ladder

The 8 keys sit along a chain of resistors between 5 V and GND. Each key connects a different point on the chain to pin A0, so each one gives a different voltage, and the Uno works out which key it is from that voltage.

Each LED

Each LED has its own Uno pin and its own resistor, which limits the current so that neither the LED nor the pin is damaged.

How to read the diagrams

Every diagram follows the same rules, so you always know what to do next:

  1. Full colour is new. Faded parts are from earlier steps and are already on your board.
  2. Each hole has a name. c4 means row c, column 4.
  3. Zoomed views show key 1. Then the overview shows all 8. Each key repeats the same pattern 8 columns further along.
  4. Labels at the edge of a board, such as D6, tell you where that wire's other end goes.
Trust the position, not the print. In this guide, row a is the row nearest the back, and column 1 is on the far left as you face the board from the front. Some breadboards are printed the other way round. If yours is, ignore its printed letters and numbers and count from these edges. The same applies to the rails: use their position, whatever colour they're printed in.

Real boards group their rail holes in fives, so a rail hole won't always line up exactly with a column. Any hole in the correct rail works.

Part A

The key bed

All of this goes on the full-size breadboard, which ends up under the keys. Everything must lie flat, under 3 mm tall, because the keys sit just 5 mm above the board. So keep leads short, press wires down, and use the kit's solid-core wires here, because tall plug ends won't fit.

1

Set up the board

You need for this step

1× full-size breadboard
1× solid wire
  1. Put the board in front of you with column 1 on the left.
  2. Note the four rails, from back to front: 5V, GND, then after the holes A0 bus and GND.
  3. Connect the two GND rails with one long wire around the left end of the board, from the back GND rail to the front GND rail.
Careful: On many full-size boards each rail is split in the middle, shown by a gap in the printed line. If yours is, bridge the gap in all four rails with a short wire. This is a common cause of "half the keys don't work".
2

Add the 8 buttons

You need for this step

8× pushbuttons
  1. Press each button across the centre channel, with its legs in columns n−1 and n+1, in rows e and f. Key 1 goes in columns 3 and 5.
  2. If a button won't sit across the channel, turn it 90°. It only fits one way round, and that's the right one.
3

Add the 8 LEDs

You need for this step

2× red
2× yellow
2× green
1× blue
1× white
  1. Lay out your LEDs in key order: key 1: red, key 2: yellow, key 3: green, key 4: blue, key 5: white, key 6: yellow, key 7: green, key 8: red.
  2. Bend each LED's legs out so they're 5 mm apart, which is 2 columns.
  3. Put the long leg (+) in column n and the short leg (−) in column n+2, both in row c. Key 1 goes in c4 and c6.
Careful: An LED in backwards won't light, but it won't be harmed either. If one stays dark in the test, turn it round.
4

Add the LED resistors

You need for this step

8× 220 Ω resistors
  1. Connect column n+2, row b to the back GND rail, for each key. Key 1 uses b6.
  2. Bend the leads so that each resistor lies flat.
5

Build the resistor ladder

You need for this step

8× 220 Ω resistors
  1. Connect the keys in a chain along the front half. For keys 1 to 7, one resistor goes from column n−1, row h to column n+7, row i. The first runs from h3 to i11, the next from h11 to i19.
  2. For key 8, the last resistor goes from j59 to the front GND rail, near column 63.
  3. Trim nothing: bend the leads to fit, and lay the resistors flat. Don't let the bare leads touch each other.
6

Connect the keys to the A0 bus

You need for this step

9× solid wires
1× 10 kΩ resistor
  1. Connect the top of the chain to 5 V: one wire from a3 to the back 5V rail.
  2. Connect each key to the A0 bus: one wire from column n+1, row g to the front A0 rail, 8 in total. Key 1 uses g5.
  3. Add the pull-down: the 10 kΩ resistor from the front A0 rail to the front GND rail, at the left end. Lay it flat, for example from column 1 on one rail to column 5 on the other.
Careful: The 10 kΩ resistor has brown, black, orange bands, and the 220 Ω ones have red, red, brown. They look alike, so check before you press it in.
Part B

Power and the Uno

The half-size breadboard shares 5 V and GND with everything else. Keep it next to the Uno.

7

Bring in the power

You need for this step

1× Uno
1× half-size breadboard
4× solid wires
  1. Connect Uno 5V to the half board's 5V rail, and Uno GND to its GND rail.
  2. Connect the half board's 5V rail to the key bed's back 5V rail, and its GND rail to the key bed's back GND rail.
Careful: Check these wires twice. 5V connected straight to GND is the one mistake in this guide that can cause real harm.
8

Add the contrast pot and backlight resistor

You need for this step

1× potentiometer
2× solid wires
1× 220 Ω resistor
  1. Press the potentiometer into the half board so that its three legs are in three different columns. The diagram uses columns 8, 10 and 12. If your pot's legs are closer together, use the columns they fit and adjust the next two instructions to match.
  2. Connect one outer leg's column to the 5V rail and the other outer leg's column to the GND rail.
  3. Add the backlight resistor: 220 Ω from the 5V rail to hole c20.
9

Connect the key bed to the Uno

You need for this step

9× long solid wires
  1. Connect each LED to its Uno pin: from column n, row a to the pin in the table below.
  2. Connect the A0 bus to Uno pin A0, from the left end of the front A0 rail.
Careful: Use the kit's longest wires. If they don't reach once it's all in the case, buy some 20 cm male-to-male wires, but only for the longest runs and only if the plug end stays clear of the keys. Check this before you close the box.

TEST NOW

Your first real test. The Uno needs the code from Upload the game. Plug in USB and run:

./maestro hwtest
  • Every LED lights once, in key order.
  • With no key pressed, A0 stays near 0.
  • Pressing a key lights its LED, and your terminal shows its name, with A0 near the values in the chart above. There's no sound or LCD text yet.

Fix anything here before moving on. It's much easier now.

Part C

The panel

The LCD, piezo and knob sit on the sloped panel, so they connect with female-to-male wires, which also let you open the box. The female end goes on the part, and the male end goes into the Uno or half board.

TEST NOW

Run ./maestro hwtest again, and turn the contrast pot slowly with a small screwdriver until text appears. If you see only a row of blocks or nothing, it's almost always the contrast, or the RS and E wires.

11

Connect the piezo

You need for this step

1× piezo
2× female-to-male wires
  1. Connect one leg to Uno D8.
  2. Connect the other leg to the half board's GND rail.
TEST NOW

Run ./maestro hwtest. Each key now also plays its note, from low До to high До.

12

Connect the knob

You need for this step

1× potentiometer
3× female-to-male wires
  1. Connect one outer leg to the half board's 5V rail.
  2. Connect the other outer leg to its GND rail.
  3. Connect the middle leg to Uno A1.
TEST NOW

Run ./maestro hwtest and turn the knob from end to end. tick= should count from 1 to 7. If it counts down, set KNOB_REVERSED to true in code/src/config.h, or swap the outer legs.

Upload the game

You need a computer with Python 3 and PlatformIO, which is easiest to get as a VS Code extension. Then get the code:

git clone -b firmware https://github.com/MishoMish/Maestro.git
cd Maestro
./maestro

A menu opens. Choose these in order, with the Uno connected by USB:

  1. Check that this computer is ready. It tells you exactly what to fix.
  2. Allow uploads to the Uno if the check asks for it. This is a one-time step on Linux.
  3. Test the wiring. This is the test used in the steps above.
  4. Upload the game.

The code has been run on a real Uno and tested extensively in a simulator. The commands have been tried on Linux so far. Windows users should run python tools\maestro_cli.py in place of ./maestro.

Test the power bank

Run the device from your bank, untouched, for 10 minutes. Some banks switch off when too little current is drawn. If yours does, try another one.

Put it in the case

Ready-to-print files and slicer settings are in the project's print folder. Print the test pieces first, to check key travel and LCD fit against your actual parts.

Assembly order

  1. Put the Uno on the tray's pegs, slide the breadboard tray onto the dovetails, and put the half board in it.
  2. Drop both trays into the back of the box. The USB jack lines up with the hole in the left wall.
  3. Put the full board in the key-bed tray, and drop it into the front of the box, pushed against the front wall.
  4. Press the key comb's 3 pegs into the deck plate, then snap the plate in with its 4 hooks.
  5. Press the LCD into the panel until the clips snap over its board, and press the piezo into its cup.
  6. Slide the knob pot into its holder, push the holder into the panel's square socket, and put the knob on.
  7. Connect the female-to-male wires, then snap the panel in.
  8. Run ./maestro hwtest once more, because wires can shift when you close the box. Then run ./maestro upload.

If something doesn't work

ProblemMost likely cause
A0 wanders even with no key pressedThe A0 wire or the 10 kΩ pull-down is loose.
Half the keys read wronglyA split in a rail (step 1), or a missing ladder link (step 5).
One key reads as its neighbourThat key's A0 wire or a ladder link is in the wrong hole.
All keys read about 1023The GND link (step 1) or key 8's last resistor (step 5) is missing.
A key's button does nothingIt's turned 90°, or its A0 wire is loose.
One LED stays darkIt's in backwards, or its resistor or Uno wire is loose.
LCD shows blocks or nothingTurn the contrast pot, then check the RS and E wires.
LCD shows random charactersThe D4 to D7 wires are swapped (step 10).
No soundCheck the piezo wires, D8 and GND.
Menu moves the wrong waySet KNOB_REVERSED to true in code/src/config.h.
Upload failsRun ./maestro check, try another USB cable, and close any serial monitor. Nothing may be on pins D0 and D1.
Keys click by themselves or never clickThe key-bed height. See btn_h in the case's guide.
